  ---                                                                                                                                 
  Analysis: variant-loop.sh vs DRQ Principles                                                                                         
                                                                                                                                      
  Your pipeline is well-structured and shares several core ideas with DRQ: iterative improvement, growing champion history, and       
  LLM-driven generation. But there are meaningful gaps between your current design and what the DRQ paper demonstrates works. I'll    
  organize these from most impactful to least.                                                                                        
                                                                                                                                      
  ---                                                                                                                                 
  1. No feedback from results to the next iteration's archetype generation

  The problem: Every iteration wipes .state/ (line 214-217) and generates archetypes blind. The archetype-creator agent has no
  knowledge of:
  - Which archetypes were tried before
  - Which ones won, lost, or by how much
  - What specific strategies the current champion already uses
  - What strategies the champions it must beat already use

  What DRQ does differently: MAP-Elites mutates existing good solutions. The search is guided by fitness. A warrior that scores well
  gets mutated to produce variations. A warrior that scores poorly is unlikely to be selected as a parent.

  Concrete fix: Persist a strategy-history.json across iterations containing each iteration's archetypes, their scores, and a brief
  description of why the winner won (or why losers lost, extracted from match data). Feed this to the archetype-creator. The agent
  prompt should say: "Here's what we've tried, here's what worked, here's what failed. Design 10 new archetypes that build on
  successes and explore gaps."

  ---
  2. No diversity preservation within an iteration (the MAP-Elites gap)

  The problem: You generate 10 archetypes, test them, pick the single best, and discard everything else. The paper's strongest
  empirical finding is that MAP-Elites (diversity preservation) is essential -- removing it significantly degrades performance (Figure
   8).

  What DRQ does differently: MAP-Elites maintains an archive of behaviorally diverse elites. Even a low-scoring variant is preserved
  if it occupies a unique behavioral niche, because it might be a crucial stepping stone.

  Concrete fix: Instead of just promoting the single best variant, maintain a behavioral archive. Define 2 behavioral axes relevant to
   Battlecode, e.g.:
  - Axis 1: Aggression level (enemy kills / total rounds)
  - Axis 2: Economy focus (VP generated + trees planted)

  Discretize these into a grid. After each iteration, place each variant into its behavioral cell. If it's the best performer in that
  cell, it becomes that cell's elite. In subsequent iterations, the archetype-creator should receive this archive and be instructed to
   generate archetypes that fill empty cells or improve existing cells, not just try random strategies.

  ---
  3. Only testing on a single map

  The problem: Line 28: MAP="${3:-MagicWood}". Running on one map is the Battlecode equivalent of DRQ evaluating against a single
  human warrior. The paper explicitly shows this produces specialists that defeat ~28% of opponents, not generalists.

  What DRQ does differently: Each warrior is evaluated against ALL previous champions (multi-opponent). Generality is measured against
   317 unseen human warriors.

  Concrete fix: Run each variant against the opponent on all 5 maps (Shrine, Barrier, Bullseye, Lanes, Blitzkrieg) or at minimum 3.
  Aggregate the scores. A variant that wins on 4/5 maps is more valuable than one that crushes on 1 map and loses on 4. This directly
  creates the generality pressure that DRQ identifies as the mechanism for producing robust strategies.

  ---
  4. No refinement variants (exploitation vs exploration imbalance)

  The problem: Every iteration generates 10 entirely new archetypes. This is pure exploration with no exploitation. DRQ's MAP-Elites
  primarily mutates existing good solutions rather than generating from scratch.

  Concrete fix: Split the 10 variants into two pools:
  - 5 "mutation" variants: Small, targeted changes to the current champion (e.g., "tweak unit ratio," "adjust aggression timing,"
  "modify spending thresholds"). These exploit what already works.
  - 5 "exploration" variants: Fundamentally new archetypes as you currently do.

  This mirrors how DRQ uses the LLM: sometimes to generate novel warriors, sometimes to mutate existing ones. The paper notes that
  most progress comes from mutation of existing elites, not de novo generation.

  ---
  5. Champion history is underutilized

  The problem: Champions are used as additional opponents to test against (line 302), which is good. But the archetype-creator and
  archetype-implementer agents don't receive the champion code or strategies. They don't know what they need to beat.

  What DRQ does differently: The growing history of opponents is the fitness landscape. Each new warrior is explicitly optimized to
  defeat all prior champions.

  Concrete fix: Include a summary of each champion's strategy in the context given to agents. Something like:
  Champion 0: VP rush with early gardener spam, wins by round 2200
  Champion 1: Soldier rush, aggressive early, wins by elimination ~1800
  Champion 2: Tank+soldier hybrid, slow build, strong late game
  The archetype-creator should explicitly be told: "Your archetypes must produce bots that can beat ALL of these strategies, not just
  the main opponent." The implementer should know what it's up against.

  ---
  6. The scoring function doesn't reward generality

  The problem: The scoring in rank-variants.sh aggregates scores across opponents additively. A variant that scores 20000 against the
  main opponent but 0 against champions could still win over a variant that scores 15000 against everything.

  Concrete fix: Use a minimum score across opponents or geometric mean rather than a sum. This forces the winner to be competent
  against all opponents, not just dominant against one. DRQ's fitness is explicitly context-dependent on all opponents simultaneously.

  Alternatively, weight the scoring: a variant must at least not lose to any champion. Any loss to a champion is heavily penalized.

  ---
  7. Parallelizing archetype implementation

  The problem: Line 267-292 implements variants sequentially. Each one requires a full LLM agent invocation.

  Concrete fix: Since variants are independent (each modifies its own folder), implement them in parallel. You could batch them into
  2-3 parallel groups to respect API rate limits while cutting wall-clock time significantly.

  ---
  8. No cycle detection or prevention

  The problem: The paper shows K=1 history produces many rock-paper-scissors cycles (77% more than full history). Your loop does
  accumulate champions, but doesn't explicitly check for cycling.

  Concrete fix: After each iteration, check if the new champion loses to any previous champion that the previous champion beat. If
  cycling is detected, flag it and bias the next iteration's archetypes toward generalist strategies rather than specialist
  counter-strategies.

  ---
  9. The copy_bot self-play is weak self-play

  The problem: Line 356 copies the current best bot to copy_bot as the opponent. This means the bot plays against a frozen copy of
  itself. But copy_bot is a single opponent -- the champion history provides additional opponents, but the primary opponent signal
  comes from this one bot.

  Concrete fix: Instead of always using copy_bot as the primary opponent, rotate through past champions or use a mixture of opponents.
   DRQ uses all prior champions simultaneously. You could run matches against the original opponent AND all champions, weighting them
  equally.

  ---
  10. No post-mortem analysis fed back into the loop

  The problem: bc17_query.py extracts rich match data (combat stats, economy curves, unit compositions, build orders), but this data
  is only used for scoring. None of it is fed back to the LLM agents.

  Concrete fix: After ranking, extract a brief tactical summary for each variant: "Lost because ran out of bullets at round 800 after
  overinvesting in trees" or "Won because early soldier pressure killed enemy archon before defenses formed." Feed these post-mortems
  to the archetype-creator in the next iteration. This gives the LLM concrete tactical feedback rather than just win/loss signals,
  analogous to how DRQ's fitness function provides gradient-like information to guide the evolutionary search.

  ---
  Priority ranking

  If I had to pick the 3 changes most likely to produce measurably better bots:

  1. Multi-map testing (#3) -- cheapest to implement, biggest generality gain
  2. Feedback loop from results (#1 + #10) -- gives the LLM the information it needs to make intelligent improvements rather than
  random ones
  3. Exploitation/exploration split (#4) -- the current approach throws away refinement opportunities every iteration

  The MAP-Elites behavioral archive (#2) would be the most impactful long-term but is also the most complex to implement properly.
